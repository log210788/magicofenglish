Part 1 • The Descriptive Word Bank

Describing Words for Theme Park Architects

💡 Click any word to copy

In themed entertainment, generic words like "big", "nice", or "old" are forbidden. Use these categorized sensory and architectural describing words to express scale, texture, atmosphere, and spatial flow:

1. Scale & Presence (Instead of "Big") Dimensions

Example: "Willys Butte stands as a towering landmark that commands the valley."

2. Texture & Rockwork (Instead of "Old/Rocky") Surfaces

Example: "Notice the striated, sun-bleached rock layers carved by desert winds."

3. Atmosphere & Light (Instead of "Bright/Dark") Illumination

Example: "As night falls, the boulevard becomes a neon-bathed, luminous spectacle."

4. Spatial Action Verbs (What Architecture Does) Kinetic Flow

Example: "The rockwork arch frames the vista and draws crowds onward."

Part 2 • The Sentence Structure Architect

4 Reusable Sentence Formulas for Alessia

Never freeze wondering how to start a sentence. Memorize these four plug-and-play architectural sentence structures:

Formula A: The Sightline Magnet (The Weenie) Wayfinding
[Spatial Location], [Architectural Anchor] [Action Verb] as a [Descriptive Adjective] beacon, naturally [Circulation Verb] guest sightlines forward.

Model: "Rising at the end of the central vista, Willys Butte stands as a monumental, sun-bleached beacon, naturally drawing guest sightlines forward into the land."

Formula B: The Immersion Barrier (The Berm) Immersion Protection
In order to eliminate [Sightline Intrusion / Outside Threat], we construct a [Height/Scale + Texture Adjectives] berm, which completely isolates [The Themed Land] from [The Real World].

Model: "In order to eliminate sightline intrusion from the freeway, we construct a 12-meter sculpted, striated rockwork berm, which completely isolates Radiator Springs from the outside city."

Formula C: Portal Transition (Compression & Expansion) Sensory Threshold
As guests pass beneath [Architectural Gateway], the space [dramatically expands / compresses], transitioning from [Atmosphere A] into [Atmosphere B].

Model: "As guests pass beneath the rustic timber entry portal, the space dramatically expands, transitioning from the crowded entrance into an amber-hued, sun-drenched canyon."

Formula D: The 3-Act Attraction Pacing Ride Cadence
In Act 1, we decompress guests with [Scenic Outdoor Float], before gliding into Act 2's [Theatrical Indoor Show Scenes], which culminates in Act 3 with [Climactic Kinetic Payoff].

Model: "In Act 1, we decompress guests with a serene canyon drive, before gliding into Act 2's show-controlled indoor dark ride, which culminates in Act 3 with a thrilling 45mph race through the hills."
