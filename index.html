Why We Switched to Kevin Rafferty’s Imagineering Method

❌ The Old Approach (Pitch Anything / Sales Frames)

Forced aggressive Wall Street closing tactics, "croc brain" dominance, and corporate financial maneuvers. For an architect with emerging English, this caused performance anxiety, unnatural posturing, and cognitive freezing.

• Hostile frame battles
• Memorizing sales psychology
• Disconnected from physical design
✨ The Imagineering Way (Kevin P. Rafferty)

Anchored in how Imagineers actually communicate: walking the listener through the space from the front entrance, pacing the story across Acts 1, 2, and 3, and building upon ideas with collaborative "plussing".

• Spatial narrative walkthroughs ("Walk through the door...")
• The 3-Act attraction architecture (Setup ➔ Show ➔ Climax)
• Marty Sklar's Law: "Tell me you can if..."
📖
Kevin P. Rafferty (Executive Imagineer & Show Writer): "Create, don’t replicate! If you’re working on something new and someone asks you to describe it and you start by saying, 'It’s like...', slam on the brakes and ask yourself: Why am I working on something that is like something else? Learn from the past, but find your own sound. Fan your spark into a flame!"

Vocabulary Clinic

The 3-Level Language Upgrade Ladder

In Imagineering, we never settle for Level 1 basic words. We climb directly from architectural description into immersive storytelling.

Level 1: Basic English

"We make a big rock so people don't see the road."

"There is an old house with dark rooms."

"The ride is very fast and scary."

Level 2: Architectural English

"We install a landscaped barrier to block external sightlines."

"A historic structure with low ambient illumination."

"High-velocity vehicle propulsion with sudden drops."

Level 3: Imagineering Storytelling ✨

"We raise a 12-meter sculpted rockwork berm to preserve 100% thematic immersion."

"A timeworn manor steeped in distressed patina and shadow-drenched corridors."

"A heart-stopping 65mph banked surge bursting through the exterior façade!"
