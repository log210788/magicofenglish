## 1. Why We Pivoted: The Imagineering Breakthrough

If you are teaching Alessia and feeling like lessons were stalling, here is the diagnosis:

> **The Problem With the Previous Method:**  
> The previous curriculum forced Alessia into Oren Klaff’s high-stakes Wall Street sales psychology (*"croc brains," aggressive frame control, investor closing*). For a creative designer with emerging English, this was disastrous. It triggered performance anxiety, forced unnatural corporate posturing, and created severe cognitive overload. Alessia was trying to juggle aggressive MBA negotiation theory while desperately searching for basic English vocabulary.

> **The Solution: The Kevin Rafferty Imagineering Method**  
> In *Magic Journey*, legendary Disney Imagineer Kevin P. Rafferty reveals how real theme park legends (Marty Sklar, Herb Ryman, John Hench, X Atencio) pitch and create:
> 1. **They walk through the space:** Imagineers don't recite financial slides. They guide the room through the physical attraction: *"Step through the entrance with me... In Act 1, we decompress the guest... As the vehicle rounds the corner into darkness..."*
> 2. **Architecture is spatial, not verbal:** Alessia's spatial intelligence is world-class. When you anchor her English to physical spaces, blueprints, lighting, and guest movement, words flow naturally.
> 3. **The environment is collaborative, not combative:** In Imagineering, creative discussions are built on **"Plussing"** (*"Yes, and how can we plus that?"*) and Marty Sklar’s rule: *"Don’t tell me you can’t because... tell me you can if..."*

## 2. The 4 Golden Rules for Coaching Alessia

### Rule 1: 70 / 30 Student-Talking-Time (STT)
If you speak more than 30% of the class, the lesson is failing. Your job is not to lecture on theme park history; it is to prompt, model, pause, and listen. If you ask a question, stop talking.

### Rule 2: The 7-Second Rule (Silence is Thinking, Not Failure)
When you ask Alessia an open question, count silently from 1 to 7 in your head before speaking. Non-native speakers must:
1. Translate your prompt,
2. Visualize the architectural space,
3. Retrieve the English vocabulary,
4. Formulate the grammatical sentence.  
*Do not interrupt her after two seconds! Give her space to build the thought.*

### Rule 3: The Recasting Technique (Never Interrupt Mid-Pitch)
Never interrupt Alessia while she is in the middle of pitching or describing a space. Let her speak for 90 to 120 seconds uninterrupted while you jot down notes.  
When she finishes, never say: *"That was grammatically wrong."*  
Instead, **recast** her thought using Imagineering language:
* *Alessia says:* "We make a big rock so people don't see the road."
* *You recast:* "I love that spatial solution, Alessia! An Imagineer would say: *'We construct a 10-meter rockwork berm to eliminate sightline intrusion.'* Repeat that line for me."
* *Alessia repeats:* "We construct a 10-meter rockwork berm to eliminate sightline intrusion."
* *Result:* Zero embarrassment, immediate vocabulary upgrade, and genuine confidence.

### Rule 4: Ban "Needy Language"
If Alessia ever starts a sentence with:
- *"Sorry for my English..."*
- *"I hope you understand what I mean..."*
- *"Maybe this is stupid, but..."*

Stop her gently with a warm smile:
> *"Alessia, strike that line! You are the lead architect in the room. Imagineers don't apologize for their vision. Start with: 'Picture this...' Let's try again!"*

## 3. Anatomy of Every 50-Minute Lesson (The Universal Clock)

Keep this exact rhythm every single session. It removes anxiety for both you and Alessia:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 00:00 - 07:00 │ Phase 1: The Warm-Up & Audio Homework Debrief│
├───────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 07:00 - 18:00 │ Phase 2: The Rafferty Story & Concept Intro │
├───────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 18:00 - 30:00 │ Phase 3: Controlled Practice & Chunks       │
├───────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 30:00 - 45:00 │ Phase 4: Simulated Imagineering Walkthrough │
├───────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 45:00 - 50:00 │ Phase 5: Recast Feedback & Next Assignment   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Phase 1: The Warm-Up (7 mins)
* **Goal:** Warm up her vocal muscles without performance pressure.
* **What you say:** *"Hi Alessia! Great to see you. I listened to your homework voice note. I loved how you described [specific detail]. What was one creative highlight in your work this week?"*

### Phase 2: The Rafferty Story & Concept Intro (11 mins)
* **Goal:** Anchor the lesson in a real Imagineering story from *Magic Journey*.
* **What you say:** *"Today we are looking at Kevin Rafferty's pitch for Tower of Terror. When Michael Eisner asked 'What are we seeing today?', Kevin put on the Twilight Zone music cassette and took them right through the front door into the boiler room. Let's look at the three terms Kevin used..."*
* **Rule:** Introduce strictly **3 to 4 core vocabulary terms** per lesson. Quality over quantity.

### Phase 3: Controlled Practice & Sentence Chunks (12 mins)
* **Goal:** Cement the plug-and-play sentence starters using the "I Do, We Do, You Do" method:
  1. *I Do:* You model the model sentence.
  2. *We Do:* You and Alessia speak it together in rhythm.
  3. *You Do:* Alessia says it independently with a new architectural example.

### Phase 4: Simulated Imagineering Walkthrough (15 mins)
* **Goal:** Put Alessia in the hot seat as Lead Architect presenting to the Disney Creative Committee.
* **Your Role:** Play Marty Sklar, Michael Eisner, Bob Iger, or an executive client.
* **The Rule:** Let her pitch for 90 to 120 seconds uninterrupted.

### Phase 5: Recast Feedback & Homework Assignment (5 mins)
* **Goal:** Deliver 2 praise points and 2 sentence upgrades.
* **What you do:** Type the upgrades directly into the Preply chat window. Remind her to complete her voice recording assignment in `homework.html`.

## 4. The "Emergency Rescue Kit": What to Do If She Freezes

When Alessia gets stuck, looks overwhelmed, or hesitates on camera:

### Tactic 1: Feed the Sentence Starter
Don't let dead silence turn into panic. Feed her the first 3 words:
> *"Start with: 'Picture this: As the guest enters...'"*  
> *(Her brain will latch onto the structure and fill in the rest).*

### Tactic 2: Offer Binary Spatial Choices
Never ask an overwhelming, vague question like *"How do you solve this master planning disaster?"*  
Instead, make it visual and binary:
> *"Would you guide guests with a central Weenie here, or with lighting along the perimeter berm?"*  
> *(Once she selects one, prompt: "Why? Start with: 'Because...'")*

### Tactic 3: Use Spatial Gestures on Screen
Hold up your hands on camera:
> *"Here is the entrance portal. Here is the bottleneck. Where do you place the main show building?"*  
> Architecture is visual. Physical hand anchors help non-native speakers unlock vocabulary.

### Tactic 4: Invoke Marty's Rule
When Alessia says *"I can't explain this in English"*, smile and say:
> *"Remember Marty Sklar's rule: Don't tell me you can't because... Tell me you can if! How can you say it using 3 simple words?"*
